1. What Is an Amyloid Fibril? The Cross-Beta Sheet Architecture

An amyloid fibril is a protein that has refolded into long, unbranched threads built from stacked beta-sheets running perpendicular to the fibril’s long axis—a structural motif called cross-beta. In a landmark 1997 synchrotron X-ray diffraction study (Journal of Molecular Biology), six different tissue-derived amyloid fibrils and two synthetic ones produced the same characteristic diffraction pattern, proving amyloid is a shared structural state rather than a single protein.

Proteins capable of adopting this fold include amyloid-beta, tau, transthyretin, antibody light chains, fibrin(ogen), and cleaved fragments of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein.

3. How ThT Assay & Microscopy Are Applied to Spike Protein & Blood Microclots

In a 2022 Journal of the American Chemical Society study, Nyström and Hammarström demonstrated that neutrophil elastase cleaves the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein into amyloidogenic peptides that satisfy three independent criteria: ThT fluorescence kinetics, Congo red positivity, and fibrillar morphology under electron microscopy. A 2025 follow-up in Biochemistry showed that fibrils from fragment Spike685 make fibrin clots markedly resistant to plasmin breakdown.

In blood-based research (Pretorius & Kell, Biochemical Journal 2022; Heliyon 2023), ThT is applied to plasma or blood smears to visualize persistent fibrin amyloid microclots. Because ThT reports on beta-sheet structure rather than amino acid sequence, a ThT blood test cannot distinguish spike-derived fibrils from fibrin amyloid—it measures the downstream structural burden.
